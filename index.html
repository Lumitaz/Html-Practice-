A Devotion · An Open Letter · A Lifelong Walk

Final · Fantasy

For every world I have wandered, every melody I have carried home. This is a love letter, written slowly, in sixteen acts.

Begin↓
I · Beginnings

The first time I held a crystal, I did not know it was a covenant.

I do not remember the first time I met you. I only remember that the world, afterwards, was wider. Somewhere between a darkened bedroom and a screen no larger than a window, you handed me a sword and a crystal and asked me to begin. I did. I have been beginning ever since.

There is a particular kind of trust a child gives an unfamiliar map. You ask them to walk, and they walk. You ask them to believe in a floating castle, in a buried sea, in a town that exists only because a stranger remembers it — and they believe. You did this to me, gently, over years. The result is that I am still walking.

II · Worlds

A cartographer of skies I will never finish charting.

Always one more island
than the map remembers.

You taught me that hope can look like four travelers in a desert, like an airship cresting a cloudbank, like an opera house lit for one. You taught me that grief has a shape, and that the shape sometimes resembles a friend laid down in still water. You showed me how a single phrase of melody, no longer than a held breath, could survive thirty years and find me again on a Tuesday, in a kitchen, doing nothing at all.

I have loved your kings and your thieves, your knight-mages and your blue mages, your engineers and your summoners and your quiet, undeclared poets. I have loved your villains for what they showed me about wanting. I have loved the way your maps always have one more island than you expect.

Every fifth or sixth time I think I have learned everything you can teach me, you change the shape of the world again. You move the camera. You set down the controller and pick up a brush. You build a city on a moon and ask me — as if it is nothing — to feel at home there. And I do. I always do.

~ · ~

A town is not a town until it has its theme. A hero is not yet a hero
until something rises beneath them and tells me, in a language
older than language, who they are about to become.

IV · Memory

What I carry, when the screen goes dark.

Whatever else I forget, I will not forget that you made me a person who listens. That when I close my eyes, I can still find the inside of a moonlit forest, or the hush before a final boss, or the held breath at the top of a tower with the wind moving through it.

I think often about how strange it is to grieve characters who never existed, in places that were never real, scored by composers I will never meet. And yet the grief is real. The places are real, because I went there. The friends are real, because I chose them. Whatever the philosophers say about fiction, you have always known better.

You taught me that loss does not undo a thing. A song that ended is still a song. A hero who fell is still a hero. A world that resets when I close the disc still asks me, the next time I return, whether I remember the way to the temple. I do. I remember the way.

The shape of a song
after the song is over.

V · Ever Yours

Thank you for the crystals. Thank you for the airships. Thank you for the small, persistent miracle of telling the same story differently, sixteen times, and never once telling it the same way twice.

Across every save file, every starting town, every closing credit —

Luna
Fin · A Devotion
